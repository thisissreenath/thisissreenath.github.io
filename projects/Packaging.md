## Overview

Gallium nitride (GaN) transistors offer high-efficiency switching capabilities, but their packages must manage heat, electrical parasitics, insulation, and mechanical reliability. This Virginia Tech project explored an embedded package design for a **100 V GaN high-electron-mobility transistor (HEMT)** intended for electric-vehicle applications.

The project was completed under the guidance of Dr. Christina DiMarino.

## My contributions

- Investigated **insulation and packaging materials** against the electrical, thermal, and mechanical requirements of the device.
- Worked on **next-level PCB thermal design**, including heat transfer paths and board-level thermal considerations.
- Contributed to bill-of-materials and process documentation for the proposed package.
- Evaluated electrical and mechanical reliability considerations alongside the team's package design analysis.

## Engineering methods

The project considered material selection, package geometry, thermal management, and electrical parasitics. Analysis and validation methods included **Ansys Q3D**, **LTspice**, and wire-bond and die-attach reliability considerations using **MIL-STD-883** test methods.

## What this work demonstrates

An engineering approach to package design that brings together electrical performance, thermal design, material selection, and manufacturability.
